DJ Matrix Returns From the Underground With a Legacy Built on Bass

Long before the current wave of electronic nostalgia turned old breakbeats into cultural currency, DJ Matrix was already making records designed to move people. In 1986, while serving in the US Marines, he independently released "Feel My Bass," a track that would eventually become closely associated with breakdance culture and competitions around the world. What began as an independent release became something considerably larger, surviving decades away from the spotlight through the enthusiasm of listeners who continued to share it, discuss it, and keep its memory alive online.

That persistence is at the heart of DJ Matrix's unusual return to music. His career was interrupted when the Gulf War began, bringing his original musical ambitions to an abrupt halt. For many artists, nearly four decades away would represent the end of a story. For Matrix, it turned out to be a pause.

 

The catalyst for his return came from an unexpected source. Egyptian Lover, a foundational figure in electro and West Coast electronic music, went looking for Matrix and eventually persuaded him to step back into the studio. Their collaboration produced "Hypnotic," released on 808 Day, 8 August 2025, and marked the beginning of a second chapter that few could have predicted when "Feel My Bass" first appeared.

There is something particularly satisfying about the way Matrix's comeback has unfolded. It has not been built solely on nostalgia. Instead, the renewed interest in his earlier work has opened the door to new collaborations and fresh music. His partnership with Beat Junkies founder J.Rocc resulted in "Feel My Bass, Reloaded," released on 22 March, a modern reworking of the track that started everything. After years of fans asking for a remake, Matrix finally returned to the song on his own terms, and by his own assessment, the new version surpasses the original.

That confidence is important because the story of DJ Matrix is not simply about recovering a lost moment. It is about discovering what an artist with decades of additional perspective can do with the sound that first gave him a name. "Feel My Bass" belongs to another era, but its continued life among breakdance fans demonstrates that the fundamental appeal of a great rhythm does not disappear with time.

Matrix's music has always been rooted in movement. The connection between his early work and breakdancing was not incidental. "Feel My Bass" became a soundtrack for dancers who needed music with physical impact, the kind of track that gives a routine momentum and makes a room respond. That relationship between sound and movement remains central to the artist's identity as he builds the next stage of his career.

Now, however, the project has expanded. House Of Neo is Matrix's new alter ego and the vehicle through which he is exploring house and dubstep. Rather than attempting to recreate the past indefinitely, the new identity gives him room to explore a different side of electronic music while retaining the instincts that have defined his work for decades.

The first House Of Neo single, "Drift Away," arrived on 5 June and established the new project. Its follow up, "Tension," moves in a noticeably different direction, suggesting that Matrix is interested in variety rather than building the entire project around one predictable formula. That willingness to shift mood and approach is particularly relevant for an artist whose history already stretches across multiple generations of electronic music.

Then there is "Road Trippin' With Angels," a track built around a very different emotional purpose. Where "Tension" introduces a contrasting atmosphere, "Road Trippin' With Angels" is positioned as an uplifting, feel good piece of music made for the open road. It captures another important part of Matrix's musical personality, the ability to make electronic music that is not solely concerned with intensity or club energy, but can also create a sense of freedom and enjoyment.
